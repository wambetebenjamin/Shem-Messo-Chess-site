<section class="container"><h2 class="text-center"> News</h2>
<div class="row"><div class="col-md-6 col-lg-4 py-3 shadow"><div class="row"><p class="text-center"><img src="https://files.chesskenya.co.ke/Content/ChatGPT Image Sep 13, 2026, 11_54_17 AM202609131158.png" height="200" /></p>
<div class="col-12 "><h4>Kenya Wins Four Medals at 2026 Africa Junior Chess Championship</h4>
<p class="text-muted">on: 03/09/2026</p>
<p class="text-muted my-0">By: Admin</p></div>
<div class="truncate"><h1 class="PDq2pG_selectionAnchorContainer" data-section-id="1tm6zb1" data-start="2424" data-end="2501">Historic Four-Medal Haul for Kenya at 2026 Africa Junior Chess Championship</h1>
<p data-start="2503" data-end="2687"><strong data-start="2503" data-end="2687">WFM Elizabeth Cassidy Maina wins historic silver and another WIM norm, while FM Jadon Simiyu claims three bronze medals and his first IM norm in a memorable championship for Kenya.</strong></p>
<p data-start="2689" data-end="2908">Kenya delivered one of its finest performances in the history of the <strong data-start="2758" data-end="2794">Africa Junior Chess Championship</strong>, returning from Bujumbura, Burundi, with <strong data-start="2836" data-end="2907">four continental medals and two important international title norms</strong>.</p>
<p data-start="2910" data-end="3130">Representing Kenya at the 2026 Africa Juniors Individual Chess Championships, <strong data-start="2988" data-end="3019">WFM Elizabeth Cassidy Maina</strong> and <strong data-start="3024" data-end="3043">FM Jadon Simiyu</strong> produced outstanding performances against some of Africa&rsquo;s strongest Under-20 players.</p>
<p data-start="3132" data-end="3378">Cassidy captured <strong data-start="3149" data-end="3196">silver in the Girls&rsquo; Classical Championship</strong>, while Jadon earned <strong data-start="3217" data-end="3262">bronze in the Open Classical Championship</strong> before adding two more bronze medals in the <strong data-start="3307" data-end="3326">Rapid and Blitz</strong> competitions.</p>
<p data-start="3380" data-end="3802">The championship was hosted by the <strong data-start="3415" data-end="3455">Burundian Chess Federation (FECHEBU)</strong> at the <strong data-start="3463" data-end="3504">King&rsquo;s Conference Centre in Bujumbura</strong> under the auspices of the African Chess Confederation. The overall event ran from 23 August to 1 September, with the nine-round Classical Championships played from 24 to 29 August. The classical competition attracted 34 players from eight African federations.</p>
<h2 data-section-id="1xk4s03" data-start="3804" data-end="3847">Cassidy makes Kenyan history with silver</h2>
<p data-start="3849" data-end="4013">At just 15 years old, <strong data-start="3871" data-end="3902">WFM Elizabeth Cassidy Maina</strong> once again demonstrated why she is regarded as one of the brightest young talents in Kenyan and African chess.</p>
<p data-start="4015" data-end="4293">Cassidy entered the Girls&rsquo; Classical Championship as the <strong data-start="4072" data-end="4087">second seed</strong>, rated 1920, behind Angola&rsquo;s WFM Jemima Paulo. She went on to score an impressive <strong data-start="4170" data-end="4198">7 points from nine games</strong>, finishing second overall and securing the silver medal.</p>
<p data-start="4295" data-end="4459">Angola&rsquo;s Paulo won the championship with 8.5/9, while South Africa&rsquo;s WFM Chisomo Boshoma completed the podium with six points.</p>
<p data-start="4461" data-end="4809">For Kenya, Cassidy&rsquo;s result carried special significance. Chess Kenya recorded it as the country&rsquo;s <strong data-start="4560" data-end="4638">first silver medal in the Girls&rsquo; Section of the Africa Junior Championship</strong>. Her performance also earned her another <strong data-start="4680" data-end="4715">Woman International Master norm</strong>, which Chess Kenya reported as her <strong data-start="4751" data-end="4770">second WIM norm</strong>.</p>
<p data-start="4811" data-end="4875">That achievement continues a remarkable 2026 season for Cassidy.</p>
<p data-start="4877" data-end="5189">Earlier in the year, she won the <strong data-start="4910" data-end="4950">African Youth U16 Girls Championship</strong>, scoring <strong data-start="4960" data-end="4969">7.5/9</strong> to finish ahead of a strong field in Uganda. The U16 title carried a WIM norm, making her performance in Bujumbura another major step towards the Woman International Master title.</p>
<p data-start="5191" data-end="5277">She had also been crowned <strong data-start="5217" data-end="5254">Kenya National U16 Girls Champion</strong> earlier in the season.</p>
<p data-start="5279" data-end="5547">What makes her latest result even more impressive is that the Africa Junior Championship is an <strong data-start="5374" data-end="5392">Under-20 event</strong>. Cassidy, born in 2011 and still only 15, was competing successfully against players several years older than her.</p>
<p data-start="5549" data-end="5734">From African U14 champion in 2025, to African U16 champion in 2026, and now Africa Junior silver medallist, Cassidy&rsquo;s progression on the continental stage has been rapid and consistent.</p>
<h2 data-section-id="lvz2n0" data-start="5736" data-end="5781">Jadon ends Kenya&rsquo;s 36-year Open medal wait</h2>
<p data-start="5783" data-end="5875">If Cassidy&rsquo;s silver was historic, <strong data-start="5817" data-end="5838">FM Jadon Simiyu&rsquo;s</strong> championship was equally remarkable.</p>
<p data-start="5877" data-end="6132">Jadon arrived in Burundi as the <strong data-start="5909" data-end="5968">second-highest-rated player in the Open Classical field</strong>, carrying a rating of 2212. Only Angola&rsquo;s CM Sonhy Jaime, rated 2214, was ranked above him at the start of the competition.</p>
<p data-start="6134" data-end="6198">And Jadon was only <strong data-start="6153" data-end="6169">14 years old</strong> in an Under-20 championship.</p>
<p data-start="6200" data-end="6513">He battled through the nine-round Classical tournament to finish with <strong data-start="6270" data-end="6279">5.5/9</strong>, taking bronze behind champion Sonhy Jaime of Angola and Uganda&rsquo;s Alvin Muhirwa. Most importantly for his long-term career, the performance earned Jadon his <strong data-start="6437" data-end="6472">first International Master norm</strong>.</p>
<p data-start="6515" data-end="6784">The bronze also ended a long wait for Kenyan chess. According to Chess Kenya, it was the country&rsquo;s <strong data-start="6614" data-end="6675">first medal in the Africa Junior Open Section in 36 years</strong>, dating back to Juma Arogo&rsquo;s podium performance in Botswana in 1990.</p>
<p data-start="6786" data-end="6809">Jadon was not finished.</p>
<p data-start="6811" data-end="6907">When the championship moved to the faster time controls, he remained among the continent&rsquo;s best.</p>
<p data-start="6909" data-end="7063">In the <strong data-start="6916" data-end="6943">Open Rapid Championship</strong>, Uganda&rsquo;s Alvin Muhirwa took gold, Angola&rsquo;s Sonhy Jaime won silver, and Jadon added another <strong data-start="7036" data-end="7052">bronze medal</strong> for Kenya.</p>
<p data-start="7065" data-end="7230">He then repeated the feat in the <strong data-start="7098" data-end="7125">Open Blitz Championship</strong>, finishing third behind Sonhy Jaime and Uganda&rsquo;s Ronald Wabwire.</p>
<p data-start="7232" data-end="7400">By the end of the championship, the 14-year-old Kenyan had collected <strong data-start="7301" data-end="7369">three individual bronze medals across Classical, Rapid and Blitz</strong>, as well as his first IM norm.</p>
<h2 data-section-id="qamrom" data-start="7402" data-end="7449">Another milestone in Jadon&rsquo;s remarkable rise</h2>
<p data-start="7451" data-end="7532">The Bujumbura performance is the latest chapter in a remarkable period for Jadon.</p>
<p data-start="7534" data-end="7835">In 2025, he became <strong data-start="7553" data-end="7590">Kenya&rsquo;s youngest-ever FIDE Master</strong>, following his victory at the Quo Vadis Open in Nyeri. Later that year, at only 13, he won the <strong data-start="7686" data-end="7723">Kenya National Chess Championship</strong>, becoming the youngest Open national champion in the country&rsquo;s history.</p>
<p data-start="7837" data-end="8082">That national championship victory also secured his place in Kenya&rsquo;s team for the <strong data-start="7919" data-end="7967">46th Chess Olympiad in Samarkand, Uzbekistan</strong>, where he now heads with another major continental achievement behind him.</p>
<p data-start="8084" data-end="8219">His performance in Burundi also represented progress from the previous Africa Junior Championship, where he narrowly missed the podium.</p>
<p data-start="8221" data-end="8304">Now he has not only reached the podium but done so three times in one championship.</p>
<h2 data-section-id="rmxjl7" data-start="8306" data-end="8355">Two teenagers, two title norms and four medals</h2>
<p data-start="8357" data-end="8458">What makes Kenya&rsquo;s performance in Bujumbura especially encouraging is the age of its representatives.</p>
<p data-start="8460" data-end="8491"><strong data-start="8460" data-end="8491">Jadon is 14. Cassidy is 15.</strong></p>
<p data-start="8493" data-end="8649">Yet the two are already competing successfully against Africa&rsquo;s best Under-20 players, collecting international title norms and setting national milestones.</p>
<p data-start="8651" data-end="8815">Cassidy is building towards the <strong data-start="8683" data-end="8719">Woman International Master title</strong>, while Jadon has taken his first significant step towards becoming an <strong data-start="8790" data-end="8814">International Master</strong>.</p>
<p data-start="8817" data-end="9128">Their performances also underline the growing strength of Kenya&rsquo;s junior development pathway. Both players have accumulated experience through national championships, African age-group competitions and international events, and that exposure is increasingly translating into results at the highest junior level.</p>
<p data-start="9130" data-end="9400">The African Chess Confederation&rsquo;s final report described the 2026 championship as one featuring exceptional depth among the continent&rsquo;s emerging players. Kenya featured prominently throughout, alongside Angola, Uganda and Algeria.</p>
<p data-start="9402" data-end="9449">For Kenya, the final tally tells its own story:</p>
<p data-start="9451" data-end="9517"><strong data-start="9451" data-end="9517">Elizabeth Cassidy Maina: Girls&rsquo; Classical Silver and WIM norm.</strong></p>
<p data-start="9519" data-end="9609"><strong data-start="9519" data-end="9609">Jadon Simiyu: Open Classical Bronze and IM norm, Open Rapid Bronze, Open Blitz Bronze.</strong></p>
<p data-start="9611" data-end="9722">Four medals. Two international title norms. Two young players continuing to raise the standard of Kenyan chess.</p>
<h2 data-section-id="s0a3xa" data-start="9724" data-end="9748">The journey continues</h2>
<p data-start="9750" data-end="9859">The 2026 Africa Junior Chess Championship will be remembered as an important chapter in Kenyan chess history.</p>
<p data-start="9861" data-end="9953">For Cassidy, it was another continental podium and another major step towards the WIM title.</p>
<p data-start="9955" data-end="10106">For Jadon, it was the end of a 36-year wait for an Open Section medal, the beginning of his IM-norm journey, and a remarkable three-medal championship.</p>
<p data-start="10108" data-end="10223">For Kenyan chess, it was further evidence that a new generation is ready to compete with the best on the continent.</p>
