The Opera Game, four chapters

Paul Morphy, Paris 1858. Seventeen moves, one argument — control of the d-file. Scroll and the board plays itself. Nothing about your wheel is touched.

Chapter 1 · Opening

Development before anything else

Morphy opens 1.e4, meets 1...e5 with 2.Nf3 hitting the e-pawn, and strikes with 3.d4 while Black is still unpacking. By move four White has three pieces out and Black has one.

  • 1.e4 e5
  • 2.Nf3 d6
  • 3.d4 Bg4
  • 4.dxe5
Chapter 2 · Middlegame

Trade on your own terms

4...Bxf3 5.Qxf3 looks like relief, but it develops the white queen for free. 6.Bc4 lands on the weak f7 square and 7.Qb3 hits b7 and f7 at once — two threats from one square.

  • 4...Bxf3
  • 5.Qxf3 dxe5
  • 6.Bc4 Nf6
  • 7.Qb3 Qe7
Chapter 3 · Tactics

Remove the defender

10.Nxb5! offers a knight to rip open the centre. 11.Bxb5+ pins the replacement, and 12.O-O-O is an attacking move — castling puts the rook opposite the black queen. Every piece has a job.

  • 8.Nc3 c6
  • 9.Bg5 b5
  • 10.Nxb5 cxb5
  • 11.Bxb5+ Nbd7
  • 12.O-O-O Rd8
Chapter 4 · Finish

Mate on the file it was always about

13.Rxd7! takes the only defender of d8. 15.Bxd7+ removes the last guard, and 16.Qb8+! forces 16...Nxb8 so that 17.Rd8 is mate. The king never leaves e8.

  • 13.Rxd7 Rxd7
  • 14.Rd1 Qe6
  • 15.Bxd7+ Nxd7
  • 16.Qb8+ Nxb8
  • 17.Rd8#
