Junior Section

Learning the game properly

Three learning tracks, a written safeguarding policy, and the resources parents need between sessions.

Learning Tracks

Three tracks, one sequence

Learners are placed by what they can do on the board, not by age. Moving between tracks is normal and expected.

Track 1

Foundation

How the pieces move, how to set up, and how to spot a piece that is simply hanging. Ends when a learner can play a full game without help.

Typically one term
Track 2

Improver

Tactics, opening principles and the basic mates. Learners start recording games and playing on a clock.

One to three terms
Track 3

Squad

Repertoire work, endgame technique and match preparation. Squad members represent the club at county and national events.

By selection
Safeguarding

How we keep learners safe

Every coach who works with a junior session is known to the club and to the school. Sessions run in shared, visible rooms, and a member of school staff is always on site.

  • Sessions in shared rooms during school hours, or with a teacher present
  • Photography and video only with written consent — see our consent form
  • No child is named in publicity material
  • Concerns go straight to the President or the academy office, in writing

For Parents

Parent resources

What you can do at home between sessions — none of it requires you to play well yourself.

Ask one question

After each session: "what was your opponent's threat?" Naming the threat out loud beats an hour of casual play.

Borrow a board

The club library lends a set for the holidays. Ask at the Saturday Academy.

The workbook

Our openings and tactics workbooks are the same ones used in sessions, so homework matches the lesson.

Enrol a learner

Registration takes about four minutes. You will need the learner's details and a payment reference.
